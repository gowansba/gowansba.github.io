Case study / Email creative

Designing for what comes after the goal.

Most post-purchase emails keep talking about the goal a customer already hit, instead of what happens next. For this one, I wanted to shift the tone completely: less "start here," more "protect what you've built."

So I leaned into a moodier, editorial look. Muted forest greens, an italic serif headline, a lot more restraint than the bright, high-energy style most supplement brands default to. It felt like a magazine spread more than a typical promo email.

I hand-coded the whole template instead of using our drag-and-drop builder, so I could actually control how the hero image, spacing, and type rendered across every inbox.

  • I handled the creative direction, the copy, and the HTML build myself
  • It ended up becoming the reference template for the rest of the retention series

Case study / Integrated campaign

Turning a hashtag into a household habit.

This one took a while to get approved, legal had a lot of notes, but it's honestly one of my favorite things I've built. The goal: get families actually engaging on Instagram and Facebook every single day for eight weeks straight, not just for one giveaway post.

I built it around three simple daily actions: move together, fuel well, track it. Nothing complicated, just habits a family could actually keep up with. I designed the full guide, a printable goal sheet, and a two-month tracking calendar, so people had something to stick on the fridge instead of just a hashtag to remember.

Weekly prize drawings kept it fun in the short term, and a running entry count built toward a bigger grand prize trip, so people had a reason to keep showing up in week six, not just week one.

  • I designed the campaign mechanics and every creative asset: the guide, the tracker, the social rules, all of it
  • Ran across Instagram, Facebook, and our owned email and SMS lists

Case study / Marketing systems

Retired the drag-and-drop builder. Wrote the emails in code instead.

When I started, the lifecycle program ran on a drag-and-drop email tool and an old SMS platform. I rebuilt both the tooling and the workflow from the ground up:

  • Moved us off Ziplingo and onto Attentive for SMS and email, rebuilding all the segmentation and automations from scratch
  • Got the team off drag-and-drop templates and into hand-coded HTML, so we had full control over how emails actually rendered on every device and inbox
  • Started using Claude Code and ChatGPT to speed up template builds, check shop links across our US and Canada campaigns, and audit creator content against our claims guidelines
  • Built a dashboard that pulls campaign and revenue data into one leadership-ready report, instead of putting that together by hand every month
