Taking a supplement brand into skincare.

In January 2026, R&D handed us an entirely new category: Precision Skincare, a three-product Skin Renewal System. My job was to turn their formulation science into language customers and affiliates would respond to, then carry that one message across every channel by launch day in September.

Timeline

Audience
Adults 30 and up who want firmer, smoother, more resilient-looking skin, plus the affiliates who share it
My role
Messaging, copy, and the channel rollout, from R&D materials to launch day
Result
Sold out within 6 hours. Our fastest launch build to date.

The hardest part wasn't writing copy. It was translation. R&D's materials were written for scientists. Customers needed to understand each product in a single sentence, and affiliates needed a story they could retell in their own words.

I anchored everything on the SPA Method™ (Signal. Preserve. Amplify.) so every touchpoint told the same story, then gave each product exactly one benefit line, written to stay inside our claims guidelines.

On launch day, the email, SMS, affiliate HUB post, and landing page all went live on that same framework. The follow-up email used real inventory to create urgency instead of inventing scarcity.

Keeping 50+ creators on brand and inside the claims.

In supplements and skincare, one overpromising sentence can turn into a legal problem. Our compliance team keeps a deep library of claims guidance, but a library doesn't help an affiliate writing a caption at 9pm. So I built tools that bring the rules to where the writing happens.

A claims checker that reads like compliance does.

I compiled our compliance documents into a ChatGPT project that reviews copy against them automatically. Paste in a caption, email, or flyer draft, and it flags risky language and suggests compliant rewrites. I run every send through it, and it backs up the claims framework our creators and affiliates follow.

The guidance itself stays internal. What's shareable is the result: copy that's safe before it ever reaches legal.

One design standard for every send.

A single written reference covering layout, type, color, button construction, and the build rules that keep emails rendering in Gmail and Outlook. It's why ten sends a week still look like one brand. A few of the rules:

  • TypeSerif headlines, sans body, fallback fonts written inline on every element.
  • ColorNeutral base. Green is an accent only, never a full background.
  • ButtonsPill-shaped, dark or light only, with an Outlook fallback so corners survive.
  • BuildEvery style inlined. Tables for layout. Letter-spacing in pixels.
  • ComplianceFDA disclaimer on every product send.

The F.I.T. Family Challenge

This was Justin Prince's challenge, and it took a while to get approved. Legal had a lot of notes. The goal: get families moving together and posting every day for eight weeks straight, not just for one giveaway post.

I built it around three simple daily actions: move together, fuel well, track it. I designed the full guide, a printable goal sheet, and a two-month tracking calendar, so people had something to stick on the fridge instead of something to scroll past.

More than 10,000 people signed up.

Weekly prize drawings kept it fun in the short term, and a running entry count built toward a grand prize trip, so people had a reason to keep showing up in week six, not just week one.

  • I designed the campaign mechanics and every creative asset: the guide, the tracker, the social rules, and the winner cards
  • Ran across Instagram, Facebook, and our owned email and SMS lists

Emails, down to the code.

Every send is hand-coded to the same standard, then tuned on opens, clicks, and what the audience responds to. Pick one to see the real design and the real code.

For subscribers. Goal: launch. Email and SMS.

The launch email had one job: introduce a brand-new category without overwhelming anyone. It opens with the system, explains the SPA Method™ in three words, then gives each product exactly one line.

The animated product GIF doubled as the launch-day SMS creative, so the two channels felt like one moment instead of two separate asks.

  • I wrote the copy from R&D's materials and built the HTML
  • Sent at 10am MT, alongside SMS, the HUB post, and the landing page

For subscribers. Goal: honest urgency.

Once launch gifts started moving, I wanted urgency that was real. The headline and progress bar reflect actual inventory, and the email says plainly that the Stack stays available after the gifts run out.

Honest urgency builds trust for the next launch. Invented scarcity burns it.

  • The progress bar is built from table cells, so it renders in Outlook too
  • Short by design: one number, one image, one button

For customers. Goal: retention.

Most post-purchase emails keep talking about the goal a customer already hit, instead of what happens next. For this one, I wanted to shift the tone completely: less "start here," more "protect what you've built."

So I leaned into a moodier, editorial look with a lot more restraint than the bright, high-energy style most supplement brands default to.

  • I handled the creative direction, the copy, and the HTML build myself
  • It became the reference template for the rest of the retention series

For customers. Goal: education. Email and video.

This one sits higher up the funnel. Instead of pushing a product, it's built around a bigger idea: muscle matters more for healthy aging than most people think, not just weight loss.

I built it around a short doctor interview instead of product photography, since the goal was to get people thinking differently before asking them to buy anything.

  • I wrote the concept and the copy, and built the template
  • Paired with a real interview instead of a straight product pitch

Retired the drag-and-drop builder. Taught the team a better way.

When I started, emails came out of a drag-and-drop tool and SMS ran on an older platform. Every send looked a little different, and each one took too long to build. I rebuilt the tooling and the workflow.

  • Moved SMS and email from Ziplingo to Attentive, rebuilding segmentation and automations from scratch
  • Took the team from drag-and-drop templates to hand-coded HTML, built on one shared design standard
  • Trained the team to use Claude and ChatGPT for template builds, link checks across US and Canada campaigns, and claims review
  • The result: 10+ sends in a work week, across campaigns, updates, and automated flows, at the same quality every time
  • Built a dashboard that pulls campaign data into one leadership-ready report, replacing a manual monthly build
